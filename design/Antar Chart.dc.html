8.78 against 100

A truthful axis flattens six years of real movement to a 3px rise at the bottom of the frame. A zoomed axis makes the trend legible and makes a 91-gigawatt gap look like a rounding error. The resolution is not to choose: draw both scales, in the same figure, and state the magnification in words.

01

Nested scale, at full width

78 verified readings, February 2020 to July 2026, each checked against its source report. The left panel is drawn to the target; the right panel magnifies the outlined band ten times. Both show the same numbers.

The dashed line is 4.34 GW per year from July 2026 to 2047, the arithmetic that reaches 100. It is division, not a forecast, and the site says so in the caption rather than leaving the reader to infer it.

Capacity arrives in steps when a reactor commissions: three steps in six years, marked on the right. A year without one is not a year behind, and nothing here says otherwise.

What changes below 480px
  • Panels stack; the connector becomes a bracket down the left and right edges, keeping the containment relationship.
  • The 91.22 measure rotates into the right margin rather than shrinking.
  • Gridlines drop from four to one. Axis labels drop to 0 / 50 / 100 and 0 / 5 / 10.
  • The rotated rate annotation is removed from the plot and moved to the caption, where it is easier to read than at 43°.
  • Nothing scrolls horizontally. A chart the reader has to drag is a chart most readers see half of.
